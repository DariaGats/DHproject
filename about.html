O. Henry and The Four Million

O. Henry was the pen name of William Sydney Porter, an American short-story writer best known for turning ordinary lives into stories full of humour, irony, and unexpected endings. His 1906 collection The Four Million includes one of his most famous stories, “The Gift of the Magi.”

Who was O. Henry?

O. Henry was born William Sydney Porter on September 11, 1862, in Greensboro, North Carolina, and died in New York City in 1910. Before becoming a successful writer, he worked as a drugstore clerk, ranch worker, land-office employee, bank teller, reporter, and cartoonist.

In 1896, Porter was accused of taking money from the bank where he had worked. He fled to Honduras, but returned to Austin when he learned that his wife was seriously ill. He was later convicted and sent to prison in Ohio, where he wrote stories to support his daughter. By the time he left prison, he had adopted the name O. Henry and had become popular with magazine readers.

O. Henry’s life was difficult in his final years because of illness and money problems, but his work remains widely read and adapted. The annual O. Henry Prize, created in 1919, continues to honour outstanding short fiction in his name.

His style of writing

O. Henry’s stories often focus on people who seem unimportant at first: shop assistants, poor couples, clerks, criminals, waiters, and immigrants. He shows that their lives can contain love, sacrifice, humour, disappointment, and surprising changes of fortune.

His writing is especially known for its surprise endings. A story may appear to move in one direction, but the final lines reveal an ironic twist that changes how the reader understands the whole situation. This technique became closely connected with his name.

What is The Four Million about?

The Four Million is a collection of 25 short stories, set in New York City. The title responds to the idea that only the “Four Hundred” were worth noticing. Ward McAllister, a leader of New York high society in the Gilded Age, said there were “only 400 people in fashionable New York Society,” meaning the people who really mattered. He published an official list of them in The New York Times on February 16, 1892. The number is also popularly said to be the capacity of the ballroom of Caroline Schermerhorn Astor, the leading hostess of the time. O. Henry argues, through the figure of the census taker, that all four million residents deserve attention.

The collection presents the city as a place full of hidden dramas. Its characters may be poor or struggling, but they still experience powerful emotions and make meaningful choices.

“The Gift of the Magi”

The best-known story in the collection is “The Gift of the Magi,” first published in the New York Sunday World in 1905 and then included in The Four Million in 1906. It tells the story of a young married couple, Della and Jim, who are poor but deeply love each other.

About this project

Why this research?

Motivation

I admire O. Henry’s insight into real things and into the emotions of everyday life and relationships.

His endings often recast everything that precedes them. I want to examine how he achieves this, using the tools of this course.

Research question

Do the endings of the 25 stories differ measurably from the rest of each story, and what does this reveal about how the surprise is built?
