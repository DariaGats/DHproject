Conclusion

What the charts show

The measurements give a mixed result. The first hypothesis is supported: in 18 of the 25 stories, the last paragraph is shorter than the typical paragraph of the same story (the typical last paragraph has 22 words, the typical other paragraph 38.5). The second hypothesis is not clearly supported: the ending has shorter sentences in only 13 of 25 stories, and the typical sentence is almost the same length (15 words in the ending and 15.6 in the rest). The third hypothesis is not supported either: dialogue is the most frequent voice of the ending, but it appears in 12 of 25 stories, which is fewer than half.

The hand coding of the twist adds a different kind of evidence. A twist was found in 22 of the 25 stories, and 15 of them passed all three tests. Six stories passed two tests, and one is a borderline case. The three stories without a twist show no clear reversal of the plot. In 12 of the 22 stories with a twist, the turning point comes before the last paragraph, so the ending gives only the answer. These features concern meaning and not form, and they are close to the definition of surprise in Bissell, Paulin and Piper (2025).

These results suggest that the form of the last paragraph explains only a small part of the surprise. Bissell, Paulin and Piper (2025) define surprise as an event that is unexpected but coherent when the reader looks back at the story. This kind of surprise depends on meaning and on the reader’s expectations, and not on the length of words or sentences. The same idea is found in Eikhenbaum (1925/1927), who describes O. Henry’s stories as built backwards, from the ending. According to him, O. Henry turns the surprise into a game with the plot scheme and the reader’s expectations, and shows his own devices openly. In this view, the ending is where the form of the short story is most visible.

In this light, the weak result for sentence length is not a failure of the method. It shows what a small-scale count can and cannot say: it can show that O. Henry’s endings tend to be short, but it cannot show how the twist works. This is why the final passages must also be read closely.
